Featured Book Project

Opening the Hospital Sanctuary

Protecting Patient and Family Rights in Times of Emergency

病院という聖域をひらく

What institutional safeguards protect patient and family rights during emergencies? Hospital visitor restrictions, the exclusion of families, entrenched infection-control measures, and caesarean sections for pregnant women testing positive for COVID-19 provide a starting point for examining hospital discretion and patient rights. The argument supports infection control while requiring evidence, time limits, exceptions, reassessment procedures, and sunset clauses whenever patient or family rights are restricted. It asks how healthcare institutions can be governed and held accountable.

02

Book Project

Villager A Becomes a Researcher

Participating in research beyond the university in the AI era

村人A、研究者になる

An unaffiliated “Villager A” reads, writes, and submits papers, participates in post-publication review, and eventually receives an invitation to peer review. As AI changes the entry points to research, this project explores how far independent researchers can participate in academic life, through a personal account of the work, judgment, and responsibility involved in reading, writing, and checking research, alongside an examination of research institutions.

Developing Book Project

Why Institutions Fail to Correct Themselves

制度はなぜ間違いを直せないのか

Healthcare, infectious-disease policy, research systems, social security, aviation, and transport: across these fields, institutions can persist without correction even after drifting from their purpose. This developing project brings the same method of primary-source investigation to the gap between institutional promises and practice, drawing on statistics, institutional design, and incentives.
